Plan before you build

Home addition or ADU: which project fits your goal?

Compare a connected home addition with a separate ADU by intended use, site access, utilities and project scope before requesting a property review.

Discuss my projectOpen the cost planner

Choose around the use of the space.

If you need a larger kitchen, another bedroom or more shared living area, start by evaluating a home addition. If the goal is a separate living space for family or another permitted use, an ADU may be worth exploring. Neither option is automatically feasible or less expensive on every property.

A home addition connects to what is already there.

Review how the new space meets the existing structure, circulation, roof, foundation and utilities. An addition may affect the rooms you already use, so discuss access, temporary protection and which parts of the house can remain occupied during work. Existing conditions can change the scope.

An ADU needs a plan for independent living.

Review the intended layout, entrance, kitchen, bathroom, utility connections and access from the street. Ask the local building department which requirements apply to your property and use. Do not assume a floor-plan size, a product listing or a nearby project proves that your lot has approval.

Compare complete project budgets.

For both options, account for design, approvals, site preparation, structural work, utilities, materials, labor and contingency. A prefabricated materials price is not comparable to a completed addition estimate until the remaining work is included. Ask who supplies and performs each part of the project.

Choose the appropriate HRG starting point.

The Home Remodel Group is the remodeling destination for kitchens, bathrooms, additions and broader renovation inquiries. HRG Prefab focuses on prefabricated panels and components with related planning and materials coordination. Those components are assembled on site. HRG Prefab is not the general contractor; the homeowner hires the general contractor for site construction and assembly.

Prepare one clear brief.

Bring your address, intended use, approximate size, photos, existing plans, utility information and target budget. Explain whether you need more shared space or a separate unit. A property review can identify what must be confirmed before you commit to a design or contractor scope.

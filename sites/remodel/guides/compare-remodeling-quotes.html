Plan your remodel

Compare the scope.
Then the price.

Two estimates can describe different projects. Put the inclusions, allowances and responsibilities side by side before deciding which price is useful.

By The Home Remodel Group · Updated November 13, 2026

Start with one project brief

Give each provider the same information: the rooms involved, what you want to change, the finishes you have selected, your target budget and the timing you have in mind. Include photos and existing plans if available. A shared brief makes gaps easier to spot.

What to compare

Budget itemAsk each provider
Demolition and preparationWhat is removed, protected, repaired and hauled away?
Materials and finishesWhich products, quantities and specifications are included?
AllowancesWhich prices are placeholders, and what happens if selections exceed them?
Design and approvalsWho prepares drawings, coordinates permits and pays fees?
Labor and installationWhich trades and finishing tasks are included?
Exclusions and unknownsWhat is not priced, and how will concealed conditions be handled?
Schedule and changesWhat milestones apply, and how are changes documented and priced?

A lower number needs context

An estimate can look lower because it leaves out fixtures, utility changes, cleanup or work that another quote includes. An allowance also differs from a fixed specification. Ask for missing details before treating two totals as equivalent.

Keep a separate record of owner-purchased items and decisions still outstanding. Confirm who measures, orders, receives and installs them. This reduces the chance that an apparently complete quote leaves work unassigned.

Separate planning from construction commitments

A cost planner provides an initial way to explore the project. Site conditions, design choices and a written construction scope are needed to refine that number. Confirm the contractor responsible for the work, the agreed deliverables and the process for approvals before signing.

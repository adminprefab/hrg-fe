Plan your remodel

Why are my remodeling quotes
so far apart?

Two estimates can describe different projects. Put the inclusions, allowances and responsibilities side by side before deciding which price is useful.

By The Home Remodel Group · Updated November 23, 2026

Start with one project brief

Give each provider the same information: the rooms involved, what you want to change, the finishes you have selected, your target budget and the timing you have in mind. Include photos and existing plans if available. A shared brief makes gaps easier to spot.

Do both garage conversion bids include the sewer hookup?

A line that says “plumbing included” may cover the fixtures and pipes inside the garage. For a Los Angeles garage conversion, ask each bidder to mark the tie-in point on the same plan. Who digs the trench, makes the connection and puts the driveway or yard back together?

Here is how two bids for the same garage bathroom can differ. The scopes below are illustrative.

ItemExample bid AExample bid B
Sewer lineInterior plumbing included. Exterior trench excluded.Trench and connection included to the point on the plan.

Ask each contractor to price the missing work or state who pays for it. Use the same scope to request a garage conversion estimate.

Does my kitchen remodel quote include cabinets and installation?

Ask for the cabinet schedule and one line for installation labor. A “cabinet allowance” can cover only the boxes. Check whether each quote includes fronts, hardware, delivery, fitting, trim and haul-away. Ask separately about countertop templating and appliance installation.

If you are buying the cabinets yourself, tell both bidders the supplier, delivery date and who will check the shipment. Ask for the labor-only price against the same layout. Plan a kitchen remodel.

A lower number needs context

An estimate can look lower because it leaves out fixtures, utility changes, cleanup or work that another quote includes. An allowance also differs from a fixed specification. Ask for missing details before treating two totals as equivalent.

Keep a separate record of owner-purchased items and decisions still outstanding. Confirm who measures, orders, receives and installs them. This reduces the chance that an apparently complete quote leaves work unassigned.

Separate planning from construction commitments

A cost planner provides an initial way to explore the project. Site conditions, design choices and a written construction scope are needed to refine that number. Confirm the contractor responsible for the work, the agreed deliverables and the process for approvals before signing.
